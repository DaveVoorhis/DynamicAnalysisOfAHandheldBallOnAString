Dynamics of a Hand-Held Ball-on-a-String — Mathematical Summary
Dave Voorhis (Derbyshire, UK) & Google Gemini
Read Full Paper ↗
Full Paper Online: https://reldb.org/c/index.php/dynamic-analysis-of-a-ball-on-a-string/ Open Article ↗

Abstract: This paper presents a rigorous physical and mathematical model of a hand-held ball-on-a-string undergoing radial reduction, demonstrating why real-world rotational velocities fall drastically short of the textbook zero-torque limit (L = m r² ω = constant). By applying Euler's Second Law (dL/dt = τnet) to a coupled, co-orbiting two-mass model, we identify hand wobble as both a primary inertial sink and a mechanical amplifier of viscous tissue dissipation. Because biomechanical damping scales with the square of the wobble radius, elastic expansion of the orbit under centripetal tension creates a quintic (∝ ω⁵) dissipative brake that bounds terminal velocity near 427 RPM (analytical upper bound) and 378 RPM (with secondary grip friction & aerodynamic drag), followed by decay to a complete stop once the inward pull ends.

1. Coupled Moment of Inertia & Centripetal Wrist Yielding:
I(r, ω) = m r² + M R(r, ω)²   where   R(r, ω) = R₀ + (m r ω²) / k_wrist
2. Quintic (∝ ω⁵) Biomechanical Viscous Damping:
τ_damp = −c_wrist · (R(r, ω) / ℓ)² · ω = −c_wrist / ℓ² · (R₀ + m r ω² / k_wrist)² · ω
3. Aerodynamic Drag (Ball + String) & Finger Pinch Skin Friction:
τ_drag_ball = −½ ρ C_d (π r_ball²) r³ ω²  |  τ_drag_string = −⅛ ρ C_d,cyl w r⁴ ω²  |  τ_fric = −μ_k (m r ω²) r_grip
4. Euler's Second Law During Pull (0 ≤ t ≤ Δt) & Post-Pull Stop (t > Δt):
I_eff (dω/dt) = τ_spin-up + τ_net   where   τ_spin-up = −2 m r (dr/dt) ω
